# Reference-inspired brand worlds

Use for requested campaign-world alternatives or a bounded revision of an existing
concept. Image remains the asset owner. Hallmark's reference-synthesis method helps
name whether each input supplies mood, craft or one specific contribution. A request
for alternatives is not a mandate to invent new brands, use a provider, generate a
fixed number of images or publish them. Keep the actual brand, offer and verified
claims unless fictional identity exploration was explicitly requested.

## Hold grammar; vary meaning and action

Inspect the supplied images before claiming visual findings. Separate reusable
relationships (medium, depth, density, light, palette relationship and type hierarchy)
from distinctive source signatures (people, poses, marks, wordmark letterforms,
objects and their exact arrangement). Reference rights do not transfer to outputs.

Choose shared constants that serve the brief. Then make each world describe a
specific meaning, environment, human ritual and motif. “Different colors” is not a
new world; “people standing in a scene” does not express what the brand enables.
The motif should arise from the ritual rather than be pasted onto unrelated scenery.
Use named closeness axes such as denser staging or softer print texture, not numeric
similarity percentages or a count of changed details as an originality test.

## Worked matrix — fictional exploration, not business claims

Suppose an approved exploration asks for three cultural campaign concepts in a
related printed family. No images have been generated or inspected for this example.
These names describe fictional concepts, not verified businesses or production marks.
Shared constants: landscape crop; tactile two-ink editorial collage; broad side light;
a quiet margin for a separately composed wordmark; people performing meaningful work;
paper-colored negative space. Keep these relationships, not a reference's exact layout.

| Concept / meaning | Environment | Human ritual | Signature motif / arrangement |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOLDROOM — a neighborhood repair workshop | Open workbench beneath a low warehouse window | Two makers align a torn coat while another passes thread across the bench | Oversized stitched seam traverses fabric scraps diagonally, ending at the repaired coat |
| TIDENOTE — a coastal field-recording collective | Wind-cut tidal steps with pools and a distant harbor | A listener lowers a hydrophone while a partner annotates a tide sketch | Nested contour lines spread from a pool, interrupting the horizontal shore rather than forming a centered halo |
| COMMONS PRESS — a community print exchange | Courtyard drying racks and an offset hand press | Neighbors trade fresh sheets along a line; one checks the inked plate | Alternating hanging sheets create a broken vertical rhythm and cast small stepped shadows |

These share medium and human scale, not scene geometry. The repair scene's lateral
hand-to-hand line, the shore scene's low open horizon and the print scene's tall
hanging rhythm should stay distinct even in grayscale thumbnail views. This is a
comparison method, not a measured similarity or originality guarantee. For one real
brand, use campaign labels instead of these fictional names, and choose only rituals
that communicate its actual offer; a staged concept is not evidence of real operations.

### Prompt spine

Write a separate concept instruction when distinct worlds are requested. Use the
available approved tool only if generation is authorized; a prompt-only handoff is valid.

```text
Asset role and placement: [campaign concept, not product/customer evidence; crop].
Actual brand and locked content: [approved identity, exact text or no text].
Input A's job: [specific visual-language traits; excluded signature arrangement].
Shared constants: [selected medium/light/density/type relationships].
World meaning: [what the offer enables, without invented results].
Environment and composition: [spatial path, layers, negative space].
Human ritual: [who acts on what, and why it expresses the meaning].
Motif: [form generated by the action; distinct placement and silhouette].
Avoid: [specific source marks, poses and arrangement; false documentary claims].
Production boundary: [concept only; exact logo/type handled separately as needed].
```

For FOLDROOM, the thread line links three hands across the workbench; the low window
provides side light and leaves the top-left quiet for type. Do not turn the seam into
a decorative floating ring, or substitute passive fashion poses: both lose the repair
ritual. Use an approved fictional label only in exploration; never manufacture a
brand's staff, customers, partnerships or measured outcomes in production materials.

## Two-image revision without losing the idea

- **Image A = visual-language reference.** Borrow only named axes, such as coarser
  print texture or deeper layering. It is not the subject/identity to copy.
- **Image B = current concept/content anchor.** Preserve its approved brand name,
  meaning, core subject, ritual and placement constraints. Preserve the previous
  version when saving a requested revision; use the project's naming convention.

Example revision instruction: “Keep Image B's repair scene, brand and hand-to-hand
thread action. Move only its surface toward Image A's coarser ink breakup; preserve
B's workbench composition and quiet type margin. Do not import A's people, motif
arrangement, exact letterforms or marks.” Inspect the result before changing another
axis. If texture changes destroy the thread's legibility, reduce that treatment rather
than rewriting the entire concept. A change in meaning needs a new concept decision,
not a silent ‘closer’ revision.

Check the requested outputs at actual placement size: readable ritual, distinct scene
geometry, shared constants, crop safety, unwanted marks and exact text. A short
wordmark may be explored in a generated image, but check spelling and occurrences.
Use the approved vector mark or reliable text overlay for production identity; a
plausible generated logo is not the actual brand asset. Report prompts versus actual
images, inspected outputs versus proposals, file versions and remaining limitations.
