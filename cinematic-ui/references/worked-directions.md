# From an art-direction sentence to a website

These are original hypothetical briefs, not researched films, real client sites,
validated templates or promised results. They show how the craft IDs can work
together. Names/copy/assets must come from the actual project. Choose one relevant
example; do not generate all of them or substitute them for the user's brief.

## A. Daylight programme — a cultural venue, not a movie poster skin

**Visitor task:** understand the venue and browse upcoming events. **Thesis:** an
open frame welcomes the visitor; a practical programme lives immediately beside it.
**Analogy:** a bright establishing shot followed by encounters. No named film has
been inspected. **Choices:** C2 + C4, T2/T4, L1, P1, M0.

### Page direction

- **Opening:** a small venue identifier and normal navigation; a large but brief
  title in a pale field; one inset original illustration or approved venue photo.
  Keep the actual “View programme” anchor close to the introduction. Dates belong
  in selectable text, not simulated lettering on the wall.
- **Programme:** a clear heading, then the project's real event list with dates,
  titles and actual detail links. A calm recurring frame gives each entry identity.
  Do not replace useful filtering with a cinematic menu or fake ticket controls.
- **Encounter:** one featured event/person with an approved portrait or labelled
  placeholder, caption and readable excerpt. This is the closer shot; it earns
  extra space because the content is specific, not because every page needs a hero.
- **Visit:** actual location/access information and existing booking/contact route.
  Keep practical links in the footer; a quiet ending is not an empty black screen.

**Proposed tokens if the project has none:** warm canvas `#f5f2e9`, ink `#252b27`,
pale panel `#e5e9de`, accent `#315845`; a system serif title and system sans support.
These are starting values, not certified contrast pairings. Check every used pair,
including muted text and focus, with the actual rendering. Use the same type roles
and frame inset across pages; never globally replace approved brand tokens.

**Narrow/still:** normal navigation wraps; title/action precede the frame; dates
and event links remain visible; caption rails fall below images. All states are
static and complete. Short pages do not need extra scenes to imitate a movie arc.

**Tradeoff:** whitespace gives attention but pushes browse results down. Shorten
the opening if visitors primarily need today's programme. **Omit:** grain, letterbox
bars, entrance animation, glass navigation, fictitious awards and automatic audio.
**Verification focus:** date/link reading order, 320px reflow, long event titles,
text resize, dark text contrast, asset-free state and actual booking destinations.

## B. Field notebook — a documentary project with depth

**Visitor task:** understand a place/process and inspect source material.
**Thesis:** move from context to a precise detail, then make room for reading.
**Choices:** C3/C4/C8, T2/T4/T5, L1 or L5, P2/P4, M0 by default.

### Page direction

- **Context:** article title, real author/date and a wide approved overview figure.
  Do not invent a “documentary” photograph using an unrelated stock landscape.
- **Detail:** a close view of an object/site feature beside a short caption that
  explains what the reader is seeing. A matching left edge links overview and
  detail even though their size and aspect differ.
- **Account:** a comfortable reading column with real subheadings, figures and
  source links. Use a margin note only if it fits; it becomes inline at narrow width.
- **Evidence:** a small set of records or process images with exact titles/dates.
  Keep an ordinary list/grid for browsing. Any tactile paper offset is decorative;
  source text and controls stay unobscured.
- **Reflection:** an attributed quote if available, or an honest concluding
  paragraph; then related records and sources. No manufactured testimonial.

**Proposed visual values:** paper `#f4efe4`, ink `#302d27`, accent `#70492e`; a readable
serif or the project's existing editorial family, plain sans captions; body measure
around 65ch. Keep originals unfiltered where documentary color matters. The visual
thesis survives with plain backgrounds and zero texture.

**Narrow/still:** crops preserve the subjects; records flatten to source order.
Nothing is revealed only on scroll. If the user asks for an enhanced wide-screen
reading view, M4 may hold a figure beside related paragraphs, with no forced pinning
or spacer tower and a normal-flow fallback at zoom/narrow height.

**Tradeoff:** contemplative pacing suits reading, not a high-density evidence search.
Keep the archive index conventional. **Omit:** auto-scrolling filmstrip, handwriting
as essential text, scene timestamps without a source, cinematic claims about real
historical footage not inspected. **Verification focus:** citations/credits,
caption association, image failure, paragraph measure, long URLs and keyboard links.

## C. Chromatic cut — a bold exhibition launch without perpetual motion

**Visitor task:** understand the exhibition and find dates, works and visiting
information. **Thesis:** the title forms a bold opening frame, then cuts directly
to useful programme detail. **Choices:** C5/C7, T1/T3/T4, L4, P5, M0 or requested M2.

### Page direction

- **Title frame:** actual exhibition title in a compact, large block with a plainly
  labelled date/action band. A color field provides energy; no stock movie imagery
  or huge video download is needed.
- **Programme cut:** a crisp static boundary to an opaque light reading surface.
  Put the schedule and practical links here. The change in density carries the cut.
- **Works:** a captioned triptych/contact sheet with real supplied imagery or honest
  placeholders. Keep selection order meaningful and links explicit.
- **Context and visit:** short curatorial text, credits, accessibility and venue
  details. Let these be ordinary, readable UI; do not make every section a poster.

**Proposed visual values:** canvas `#f2eee4`, ink `#1b2524`, opening field `#e7fa65`
with dark text; the accent appears in small wayfinding details later, not every
panel. Use a bold available sans family for the title and its regular weight for
support. Fit real words and localization before deciding fixed line breaks.

**Narrow/still:** scale the title with rem-aware fluid type and wrapping, stack the
works, retain dates above the fold when practical. A requested work-image update
may use one finite M2 change; it is not an auto-playing slideshow. Normal links
work without JavaScript. Reduced motion changes state immediately.

**Tradeoff:** graphic impact can crowd long titles and detailed content. Reduce
scale rather than truncating the exhibition name. **Omit:** glitch, rapid flashes,
cursor replacement, infinite marquee and required animated letters.
**Verification focus:** title at 200% text size, line-height/diacritics, contrast
on the accent field, source-ordered captions and all real destination links.

## D. Lit object — a cinematic portfolio that is more than a glowing hero

**Visitor task:** inspect a maker's work and understand material/process.
**Thesis:** a single lit subject opens the page; close, factual frames explain it.
**Choices:** C2/C1/C6, T3/T4, L3/L6, P3, M0 or finite M1 on decoration only.

### Page direction

- **Stage:** one approved object image or original abstract shape inside a bounded
  dark frame. Keep a concise title, description and real project anchor outside
  the busiest lighting area. A radial highlight is behind the subject, not the text.
- **Close study:** move to a lighter opaque section with one detail image, honest
  dimensions/material notes if supplied, and an explanation. This change in scale
  prevents the page from being merely dark panels with repeated glow.
- **Process pair:** two captioned views that show real making decisions; no invented
  specifications, performance data or “before/after improvement.”
- **Collection/contact:** an ordinary set of project links and an existing contact
  route. Do not create a fake enquiry success state or nonfunctional purchase button.

**Proposed visual values:** stage `#17201e`, stage text `#f5f1e7`, detail canvas
`#eee9dd`, detail ink `#222b27`, warm decoration `#cf9a60`. The glow is decorative,
not an approved text color. Choose a system sans or approved project face; a quiet
serif project name can be considered only if it supports the actual brand.

**Motion option:** if requested, specify M1 as a one-time light cue with an exact
maximum duration, small travel and static endpoint. The object and text do not move.
No loop survives offscreen, hidden document, reduced-motion change or view teardown.
Failure/missing APIs simply retain the designed still. Measure rather than assume
that gradient/transform animation is cheap.

**Tradeoff:** the atmosphere needs strong subject framing; a weak/no image should
not trigger a download of unlicensed film imagery. An original abstract illustration
must be labelled when it could be mistaken for a real product. **Omit:** all-page
blur, fake metrics, magnetic buttons and unnecessary 3D dependencies.
**Verification focus:** stage text/focus contrast, light-surface transition, image
fallback, no-JS completeness, preference changes and optional-feature failure.

## Extend to a site without inflating the workflow

Use a small shared grammar: frame inset, title/caption roles, spacing scale, light
logic and normal navigation. Homepage establishes the world; detail pages explain
one subject; programme/archive pages help people find content; contact pages keep
real forms, validation and submission paths. Reuse components and motifs rather
than forcing different wireframes or entrances across every route. A component-only
request can use just a frame/type adjustment and its relevant checks.
