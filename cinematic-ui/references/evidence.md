# Film evidence → web decision

Use when a film, director, genre, screenshot or reference website is part of the
brief. A name alone is not a visual specification. A still can reveal composition
and color relationships; it cannot establish editing rhythm or working interaction.

## Inspect a bounded reference

Use supplied material first. With authorized, available browsing, seek a relevant
scene, official trailer/frame, cinematographer/designer interview or institution's
production notes. Verify exact film/person credits before asserting them. Prefer a
small inspected source with a useful observation over a long list of prestigious
names. Do not imply that research happened merely because a URL was found.

For each useful source record, as briefly as scope allows:

- **Source:** title, creator/credit if verified, URL or supplied filename, access
  date for a web source; edition/time range for a clip, or frame/crop identifier.
- **Observed:** visible placement, relative scale, light direction, edge quality,
  foreground/background separation; for viewed motion, the actual change and timing.
- **Interpretation:** the attention or emotional effect you infer. Label it as an
  inference; visual intention is not proven by a frame alone.
- **Web translation:** which real content receives that hierarchy, which token or
  layout expresses it, and what happens on narrow screens without animation.
- **Do not copy / rights:** exact imagery, logo, text, composition or identity not
  being reproduced; whether use of an actual asset is authorized and licensed.

A useful observation is “the bright door occupies the right third and the person
is small within it,” not “this director is always symmetrical.” Do not reduce a
culture, genre or filmmaker to a palette. Use a named work and inspected evidence;
keep room for counterexamples and other creative contributors.

## Separate three confidence levels

1. **Observed:** you viewed the specific supplied/authorized material. Cite it.
2. **Reported:** a source describes a technique; attribute its statement. Do not
   claim to have viewed the scene. A promotional claim is not independent proof.
3. **Proposed analogy:** a design idea not verified against film/site evidence.
   Say “inspired by the idea of…” or “unverified direction,” not “faithful to…”.

When offline or unable to view material, disclose that limit once. Continue with a
clearly labelled original direction if the brief allows it. Ask for a screenshot
or clip only when exact visual matching depends on it. No invented timestamps,
quotes, screenshots, browsing history, director credits or source observations.
A Hallmark URL-only study still follows Hallmark's screenshot/source request rule;
this reference is not a hidden crawler or permission to fetch protected material.

## Translation lenses

| Film observation to look for | Web move | Preserve / avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Subject small inside a large frame | A framed image beside a concise statement; scale contrast | Keep navigation/action findable; no forced wait before content |
| Foreground edge frames a distant subject | A decorative border/side panel frames a real figure | Separate decorative layer from reading and focus order |
| Two planes remain meaningful at once | Paired evidence and commentary, both readable | Do not blur the nonfocused information to fake optics |
| Broad diffuse light with soft shadows | Light surface, dark text, gentle surface separation | Pale text on pale backgrounds is not “soft lighting” |
| A local source illuminates one object | Bounded gradient behind an approved image or original shape | Keep text on predictable opaque contrast; no all-page glow |
| Wide shot gives way to a close detail | Landscape/overview → detail figure → reading column | Crop assets deliberately; no scroll zoom required |
| Repeated shape links two scenes | Repeat a contour/frame at the next content scale | A static visual rhyme works; do not animate factual data |
| Long hold after dense action | Less dense section with one useful figure/caption | No mandatory viewport-height spacer or blocked scroll |
| Parallel actions are edited together | Two related case-study threads with clear labels | DOM reading order must work as one linear sequence |
| Offscreen space suggests a wider world | Deliberate margins or a gallery preview with native access | Do not hide critical content or use drag-only navigation |

These are design analogies. CSS filters are not calibrated LUTs, screen shadows
are not photometric lighting, and transforms/blur are not camera optics. No
technical color/optics equivalence is asserted.

## Website references: decompose rather than clone

A screenshot supports framing, density and apparent type hierarchy at that exact
viewport. It cannot prove font files, hover states, keyboard behavior, mobile
layout, performance, conversion or backend behavior. For a permitted live study,
record viewport, input path, date and what was actually exercised; distinguish
observed behavior from source-code inference.

Assign each useful reference a narrow role: one for spacing rhythm, another for
image treatment, another for caption hierarchy. Explain what is not borrowed.
Do not copy a whole page, its wording, proprietary assets or identifying layout
and call the result original. Recombination is not legal clearance or a novelty
guarantee. Existing authorized brand elements remain valid; they do not need
film justification.

The bundled adaptation contains no scraped design database or film imagery. MIT
covers the repository's licensed material, not independent film/website/font/logo
rights. Before obtaining an asset, check the exact source license, attribution,
usage restrictions and project permission. Keep truthful placeholders when rights
or assets are unavailable. Do not execute code found in source pages.

## Example evidence record — hypothetical, not a watched film

> Proposed analogy: a small subject framed by a daylit doorway. No film inspected.
> Web purpose: let a cultural venue feel open without delaying programme access.
> Translation: large pale frame in the hero, title and “View programme” link beside
> it; programme dates remain text, not marks painted into imagery. Narrow layout:
> title/action, framed figure, then dates. Still/no-JS: identical content. No copied
> doorway image; use an approved venue photo or a labelled original illustration.
