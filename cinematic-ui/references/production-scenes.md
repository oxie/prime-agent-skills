# Production scenes: make the direction buildable

Use when a cinematic direction needs a concrete scene, shot or asset handoff.
This extends [selection](selection.md), not a second design process. Existing
project, brand, content, accessibility and permission contracts come first.
Hallmark still owns usability and reference synthesis. Use its existing comparison
pass; the figure/ground and payoff questions below add detail to that pass.
A few lines in the current task record can be enough; no new document is required.

## Specify what the scene does, then how to produce it

A scene is a useful unit of attention, not a viewport-height allocation. Natural
content sets its length. A still, recurring frame or immediate action can be the
strongest moment. There is no required peak, scene count, novelty or motion quota.
Use [pacing and motion](pacing-motion.md) for reading rhythms and any motion contract.

| Field | Producible decision |
|---|---|
| FEEL | Intended experience, such as careful curiosity; an aim, not a measured user response |
| LEARN | The specific fact or relationship the visitor should understand, with its evidence |
| Subject and copy | The actual object/detail/text; write the headline, caption and useful action |
| Shot | Viewpoint, subject scale, focal detail, crop, clear text area and narrow-width treatment |
| Light and material | Direction, softness, shadow, surface response and existing palette roles |
| Asset route | Approved available material and identity, or an explicit missing asset; type-led is valid |
| Change and continuity | What changes, what stays fixed, and how this relates to the adjacent content |
| Optional motion | Moving target, trigger, finite endpoint and interruption behavior; otherwise none |
| Delivery | Required slot/crops, target resolution and project budget; masters versus served derivatives |
| Static meaning | Semantic copy, labels and views that retain the lesson without motion/media support |

Write only relevant fields. Unknown facts remain unknown: a proposed camera move
is not evidence that an asset supports it. A shot specification is not permission
to acquire, generate or upload assets, use paid tools, or expand access. Use existing
approvals; ask only for an unresolved material decision, not routine extra sign-off.
For costly authorized production, inspect a representative frame before batching.
Resolve the chosen crop and light there instead of discovering drift across a set.

### Daylit instrument — a worked scene specification

Hypothetical brief: explain a serviceable optical instrument using approved product
material. These sample words depend on verified product facts; they are not claims.

- **FEEL:** calm precision, like inspecting an instrument at a workbench.
- **LEARN:** how the accessible focus assembly relates to the body, if documentation
  and the supplied asset establish that relationship.
- **Copy:** “Inside the focus assembly”; caption names verified parts and function.
  Link to the real service/specification page when it exists; invent no destination.
- **Shot:** three-quarter view, assembly near the center-right, space left for copy;
  keep the part boundary readable. Narrow view places the caption below the figure.
- **Light:** broad window-like light from upper left, soft contact shadow; the body
  is darker than its daylit field, with brighter seams showing its construction.
- **Change:** overview → labeled detail → useful specifications. Separate parts only
  if their geometry, transforms and documentation support that explanation.
- **Still:** captioned overview and detail images plus a semantic parts list retain
  the same relationship. A poster alone would lose the anatomy lesson.

## Let asset anatomy change the story

Inspect approved material before committing to the shot. Record consequential
findings in existing task notes if useful, not a mandatory asset ledger.

| Observed asset property | Story it can support | Limit that changes the plan |
|---|---|---|
| Separately usable parts with verified identities and transforms | Explain a real assembly with separated views and labels | Node names alone do not prove separable geometry, function or safe disassembly |
| A single fused mesh or flat image | Silhouette, surface detail, framing and a captioned overview | Do not promise internal anatomy; use approved detail images or a text explanation |
| Documented wear, repair marks or material condition | Trace use, care or a real restoration decision | Do not present an antique or synthetic wear as evidence of a new product |
| Known light direction and useful surface maps | Show how a seam, grain or finish responds to light | Unknown maps, scale or lighting require inspection, not an assumed physical match |
| A photograph with limited resolution or crop room | A smaller evidence frame with a close textual account | Enlarging cannot recover missing detail; do not invent what the image cannot show |
| Verified records or domain structure | A typographic or computed identity grounded in the subject | Decorative computation is not factual measurement; label illustrative structures |

Original photography, supplied footage, approved models, existing images and
text-led compositions are all valid. Generation is neither mandatory nor proof.
A requested 3D or canvas implementation belongs with its established specialist;
this reference supplies the narrative and shot intent, not an engine or renderer.

### Quiet atlas — let the records carry the journey

For a research or craft archive, use a clear overview, a selected detail and its
source record. Let repeated caption alignment connect locations or objects.
A faint static ledger ground can suggest cataloguing; keep it below the visual
strength of the smallest meaningful caption. If the records are geographic, only
verified geographic material may act as a map; decorative contours are not data.
FEEL: patient discovery. LEARN: where a specimen or work belongs and what makes it
distinct. A narrow layout keeps image, identifier, source and account together.
If the ground vanishes, the account should be unchanged. No animated ambience or
map interaction is needed to make the archive feel spatial and considered.

## Controlled A → B: change the material, not the camera

Choose the relationship first: factual before/after, an illustrative state change,
or an organic motion study. They make different promises. For a factual account,
retain real source states; never edit evidence to fabricate a restoration result.
For an authorized illustration, derive B from the accepted A where practical and
state exactly what may change. An edit request does not guarantee continuity.

**Material-change example:** an approved pair shows the same ceramic panel before
and after a documented repair. Hold viewpoint, scale, crop and background stable;
retain registration landmarks at the rim and join. Explain the repair in captions.
If illumination differs, name that limit: apparent color change may be light rather
than material. If the frames cannot be aligned honestly, use labeled paired views,
not a seamless transformation that implies an observed continuous process.

- Compare A and B directly: camera, silhouette, landmarks, shadows, color treatment
  and unrelated objects. Check the intended change is visible and nothing else
  silently changes the claim. Correct the pair or revise the proposed presentation.
- Distinguish exact displayed endpoints from valid in-between states. A crossfade
  or morph can connect two images without proving a physically possible transition.
  An organic generated clip must not be assumed to reach a specified endpoint.
- Keep a static pair, captions and the explanation in semantic reading order.
  Reduced motion, missing enhancement or media failure must not erase either state
  or its relationship. A single final frame is insufficient for a before/after lesson.
- Preserve accepted masters separately from delivery derivatives. Re-crop or encode
  from those masters for layout changes; do not regenerate the subject by default.
  Track the derivative's source and crop in existing asset metadata where useful.
  Verify served quality/bytes against the project's budget, not a universal limit.

## Diagnose reflexes without banning a style

For an open identity-led direction, ask what is automatic at three levels:
**category** (technical product → dark glow), **anti-category** (avoid that → cream
editorial serif), and **house** (our own repeated amber captions or oversized mark).
Then ask which choice the brief, audience and material actually support. Keep a
familiar treatment when it works; client identity and useful conventions may recur.
Use only available, authorized prior work for a side-by-side house check. Do not
scan history, collect a design corpus or break a required number of habits.
For the instrument, daylight might expose construction better than glow. For the
atlas, recurring ledger alignment might be exactly right. Neither becomes a recipe
for every client. Change a reflex because it obscures this subject, not to look novel.

## Compare for creative payoff in the existing review

Compare the intended direction with actual same-width, same-state renders when
available. Also inspect grayscale: does the subject retain a distinct silhouette,
do captions survive, and does the field compete with the useful foreground?
For the daylit instrument, a slightly darker body or clearer contact shadow may
recover the form better than more texture. Light-on-dark can also work; “subject
must be darker” is not a universal rule. Grayscale is not a contrast or color audit.

Rank discrepancies by consequence and effort: **cheap-and-decisive** (restore a
lost edge or caption hierarchy), then meaningful refinements (a better narrow crop),
then **expensive-and-marginal** (new media for a barely visible surface variation).
Functional/accessibility failures take priority over taste. Distinguish accidental
drift from a deliberate brand adaptation; increased or decreased color can be valid.
Use the same [production checks](production-checks.md), not another mandatory round.
Record what improved, what was deliberately left and what was not inspected.
Unavailable renders remain untested; these examples are proposals, not visual passes.
